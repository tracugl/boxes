Servo Turnout Drive

A hobby servo throws the points from beside the subway, not under the turnout. A single music-wire rod runs in a tube tight under the deck, crosses the subway's train envelope, and turns up through a slot into the turnout's tiebar. An omega loop bent into the wire holds the blades closed and takes up the servo's overtravel.

Drawn for N (3 mm ply, subway at 23.8 mm). HO differs only in the wire sizes and clearances in the table below. All dimensions are in millimetres. Servo dimensions are typical SG90 figures, so measure yours before cutting.

Servo height. Four 3 mm spacers (12 mm) hang the bracket plate so the horn turns just under the deck, level with the rod. At mid-throw the horn arm points into the page, so its hole sits over the shaft in this view.

Frog switch. The microswitch (11) sits beside the wire just under the turnout, screwed to a small ply block (13) glued to the deck, and stays clear of the subway corridor. A small kink (12) bent into the wire, 2 mm sideways towards the switch, presses its lever: no extra parts and nothing to solder. The kink is before the omega loop, so it moves exactly with the blades, not with the servo. (In this view the kink points towards you, so it doesn't show.) Frog switch placement. The switch and its block sit just under the turnout, on the side of the wire away from the servo, and stop 1.5 mm short of the subway corridor. The kink sits under the switch, about 14 mm from the slot near the lever's hinge, and stands 2 mm out towards the switch. When the wire moves towards the turnout, the kink glides along the underside of the lever from the hinge towards the tip, so it rides over the lever rather than butting into it, and presses it about 1 mm. Moving back, it slides clear and the switch releases. The tube starts after the switch, at 24 mm, and the first clip is clear of it.

Servo position. The servo's shaft is 10 mm off the wire's line, so at mid-throw its horn arm points straight across to the wire. Keep the bracket at least 20 mm from the corridor: the servo body hangs below the trains' roof line.

Wiring

One Arduino Nano and one PCA9685 board drive up to 16 turnouts. A toggle switch per turnout sets its position.
Sheet 5 / 5
+5 VgroundI²C (SDA, SCL)signal

Power the servos from their own supply, into the PCA9685's V+ terminal. Each servo can draw half an amp or more while it moves, more than the Nano can supply. The board joins its two grounds, so the supply and the Nano share a common ground.

Toggles: each switch connects its Nano pin to ground. The sketch uses the internal pull-ups, so a closed switch reads as one position and an open one as the other. Frog switch: the frog wire goes to the microswitch's common, and the two rails to its contacts. It's independent of the Arduino and works on DC or DCC.

Fitting a turnout

  1. Lay the turnout, then cut the deck slot (2) under its tiebar hole: 3 mm wide for N, 4 mm for HO, and T + 6 long across the track.
  2. Glue the spacer stacks (10) and the bracket plate (9) under the deck, outside the subway corridor.
  3. Set the points halfway across. Power the servo at 90° and fit the horn so its arm points square to the rod.
  4. Bend the drive wire (3): a hook over the tiebar, the drop through the slot, the run, an omega loop (6) near the servo and a 3 mm Z-bend into the horn. Thread the run through its tube (4).
  5. Glue the tube clips (5) under the deck every 80–100 mm, with the tube tight against the deck where it crosses the subway.
  6. Tune the two end angles in the sketch: move each end until the blade closes and the omega loop just starts to flex.
  7. Live frog: bend the kink (12) into the wire about 14 mm from the slot, so it sits under the lever near its hinge, 2 mm out towards the switch side (do this before threading the wire), glue the switch block (13) under the deck beside the wire, and screw the microswitch (11) to it with its lever against the kink. Slide it until it changes over just before the blade closes, and wire it as on sheet 5.

Arduino sketch

Moves each servo slowly, then switches its signal off so it doesn't buzz. Check the library calls against the version you install.
// Turnout servos on a PCA9685, one toggle switch per turnout.
// Library: Adafruit PWM Servo Driver.
#include <Wire.h>
#include <Adafruit_PWMServoDriver.h>

Adafruit_PWMServoDriver pwm;

const uint8_t COUNT = 4;
const uint8_t SWITCH_PIN[COUNT] = {2, 3, 4, 5};   // toggles to ground
const int ANGLE_A[COUNT] = {80, 82, 79, 81};      // tune per turnout
const int ANGLE_B[COUNT] = {100, 98, 101, 99};
int angle[COUNT];

// Pulse width (µs) for an angle: 0–180° → 500–2500 µs.
int pulseFor(int deg) { return map(deg, 0, 180, 500, 2500); }

// Move one servo a degree at a time, then switch its signal off.
void moveTo(uint8_t ch, int target) {
  while (angle[ch] != target) {
    angle[ch] += (target > angle[ch]) ? 1 : -1;
    pwm.writeMicroseconds(ch, pulseFor(angle[ch]));
    delay(20);
  }
  delay(100);
  pwm.setPWM(ch, 0, 4096);   // fully off: no buzz, no jitter
}

void setup() {
  pwm.begin();
  pwm.setOscillatorFrequency(27000000);
  pwm.setPWMFreq(50);
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < COUNT; i++) {
    pinMode(SWITCH_PIN[i], INPUT_PULLUP);
    // Start where the toggle says, without sweeping across.
    angle[i] = digitalRead(SWITCH_PIN[i]) ? ANGLE_A[i] : ANGLE_B[i];
    pwm.writeMicroseconds(i, pulseFor(angle[i]));
    delay(300);
    pwm.setPWM(i, 0, 4096);
  }
}

void loop() {
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < COUNT; i++) {
    int target = digitalRead(SWITCH_PIN[i]) ? ANGLE_A[i] : ANGLE_B[i];
    if (target != angle[i]) moveTo(i, target);
  }
}
